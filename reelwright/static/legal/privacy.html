Reelwright Privacy Policy

Effective 23 November 2026 · Reelwright is provided by [YOUR NAME OR COMPANY] · Contact: [CONTACT EMAIL]

Reelwright is a video editor and clip-publishing app that runs on your own computer. This policy explains what information Reelwright handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. In short: Reelwright has no servers of its own and does not collect, sell or share your information with us. Your videos, transcripts, settings and account tokens stay on your computer, and are sent only to the services you choose to connect, when you use a feature that needs them.

1. Information stored on your computer

Reelwright stores the following in its data folder (on Windows, %LOCALAPPDATA%\Reelwright for the installed app, or the data folder when run from source):

  • Your media and edits: videos you import or receive from OBS, thumbnails, rendered clips, cover images, music you add, transcripts (speech-to-text), detected audio, scene, on-screen activity and chat signals, and your clip edits. These project files live in the data folder's projects folder, or in another folder you choose under Settings › Storage (for example on a second drive); your clip edits themselves stay in the data folder.
  • Space saver archives: if you turn on the space saver's archive mode, past-due videos and renders are moved to the archive folders you choose, each with a small .json file holding its title, caption, hashtags and post links. In delete mode they are deleted from your computer instead. Nothing is archived or deleted unless you switch the space saver on.
  • Settings and credentials: your Anthropic API key, keys for other AI providers you add, the OAuth tokens for the YouTube, TikTok and Instagram accounts you connect, OBS WebSocket passwords you enter, the pairing token for receiving clips from other PCs, and one signing key per phone you pair with the phone remote (with the name you gave the phone and when it was last seen), and the Client ID and Client secret of a Twitch app if you add one for viewer clips (Settings → Signals). These are stored unencrypted in files that, by default, only your user account can read; protect your computer accordingly.
  • Live chat log: if you name a Twitch channel under Settings → Signals → “Record my Twitch chat live”, the public chat messages of that channel are saved while Reelwright runs, each with the time Twitch sent it and its text but no username (chatlogs folder). They are used only to measure chat reactions in your own recordings and VODs. Clear the setting to stop; delete the folder to remove what was saved.
  • Learning memory: to improve clip picks, Reelwright keeps a record of your feedback — clips you rate, publish or delete — including each clip's title, up to 1,500 characters of its transcript, measured traits (length, loudness, pacing), your edit choices, and the view and like counts of your published posts. This record is kept even after you delete the clip or project, so the lessons are not lost. You can export it, import it or delete all of it at any time (Virality Lab → “What Reelwright has learned”).
  • Diagnostics: a local event log (events.log, rotated at 2 MB) and a record of AI usage per provider (token counts and estimated cost per feature, without prompt contents).

Speech transcription (Whisper), on-screen activity analysis and face detection run entirely on your computer. Reelwright contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code and sends no usage data to us.

4. How long information is kept

Everything stays on your computer until you delete it. Deleting a project removes its media, transcript and clips; the learning memory is kept until you reset it. Uninstalling the app does not delete the data folder; delete that folder to remove everything. Data already sent to a third-party service (for example, a published video) is kept by that service under its own policy.

5. Your choices

  • Disconnect any account on Reelwright's Publish page; this deletes its stored tokens from your computer.
  • Revoke access at the source as well: Google at myaccount.google.com/permissions; TikTok under Settings → Security → Manage app permissions; Meta under Settings → Apps and websites.
  • Remove your API key in Settings to stop all Claude requests.
  • Reset, export or import the learning memory in Virality Lab.
  • Turn off the OBS network receiver at any time on the OBS link page.
  • Turn off the phone remote, or revoke a paired phone (it stops working at once), in Settings → Phone remote.

6. Security

Reelwright's main interface can only be reached from your own computer. The optional network receiver for OBS clips accepts only signed, time-limited requests, can only create new projects, and never publishes the clips it receives publicly. The optional phone remote listens separately, is off until you turn it on, refuses addresses outside your private network, accepts only requests signed by a phone you paired (each one usable once), proves its answers to the phone, and serves videos only through links signed for that phone that expire after 30 minutes. It uses plain HTTP on your network, so anyone on that network can see that the traffic exists and what it contains (titles, previews), but cannot read the phone's key or act as the phone. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and your credentials are stored on your computer, so keep your computer and accounts protected.
